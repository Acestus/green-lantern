# Drywall Repair Guide — 31 in × 12½ in Wall Opening

**Project:** Patch the rectangular drywall opening in the wall
**Prepared for:** Madeline
**Photo measurements:** approximately **31 inches wide × 12½ inches high**
**Important:** Measure the drywall thickness and each opening bay before buying materials. The photo suggests a center stud and framing around the opening, but it does not prove the drywall thickness.

## Before buying anything

1. **Check for electrical wires, plumbing, or other hazards.** Do not drill or cut farther into the wall. If you see wires, pipes, water, mold, or significant structural damage, stop and ask for help.
2. **Measure the drywall thickness at the exposed edge.** Common interior drywall is ½ inch, but buy the thickness that actually matches the wall. A scrap of the old drywall can be taken to Ace Hardware for comparison.
3. **Measure each bay separately.** Because the opening has a center stud, do not assume the two halves are identical. Measure the left and right openings at the top, middle, and bottom.
4. Plan to cut each replacement piece about **⅛ inch smaller** than its opening in both width and height. A tiny gap is easier to fill than a piece that will not fit.

## Recommended repair method

Use two drywall patches—one for the left bay and one for the right bay—rather than trying to cover the center stud. Each patch should fasten to the existing wood framing on all four sides where possible.

### Step-by-step

#### 1. Protect the room

- Move furniture away from the wall.
- Put down a drop cloth.
- Wear safety glasses and a dust mask, especially while cutting or sanding.
- Keep children and pets away from the work area.

#### 2. Clean and inspect the opening

- With a utility knife, carefully trim loose, ragged, or paper-covered drywall so the edges are solid and reasonably straight.
- Do not enlarge the opening unless necessary.
- Look inside with a flashlight. Confirm that the replacement screws will go into wood framing, not into something hidden.

#### 3. Measure and mark the patches

- Measure the left bay and right bay independently.
- Write the measurements down.
- Mark the measurements on the new drywall with a pencil and square.
- Subtract about ⅛ inch from each dimension so the patch has a small, even gap.
- Label each piece “left” and “right.”

#### 4. Cut the drywall

- Score the paper face several times with a sharp utility knife along the straightedge.
- Snap the board away from the score line.
- Cut the paper backing from the other side.
- Clean the cut edge with a drywall rasp or sanding sponge.
- Test-fit each piece. It should slide in without force and should not rock excessively.

#### 5. Fasten the patches

- Place the left patch into its opening and hold it flush with the existing wall surface.
- Drive drywall screws into the surrounding wood framing, usually about 6–8 inches apart, keeping screws at least ⅜ inch from the drywall edge.
- Do not overtighten: the screw head should be just below the paper surface without breaking through it.
- Repeat for the right patch.
- If any edge has no solid wood behind it, stop and add suitable drywall backing rather than leaving that edge unsupported.

#### 6. Apply joint tape and compound

- Brush or vacuum away dust.
- Apply a thin coat of premixed joint compound over each seam.
- Press paper joint tape or self-adhesive mesh tape into the compound over every seam.
- Cover the tape with another thin coat, extending it several inches beyond the seam.
- Cover screw heads with a thin coat.
- Let it dry completely according to the container directions—usually overnight.

**Spackle note:** Spackle is fine for tiny nail holes, but joint compound is the better choice for these larger seams. If the available spackle says it is suitable for drywall seams and can be applied in thin layers, it may work, but buy joint compound if in doubt.

#### 7. Add additional coats

- Lightly scrape off ridges with the putty knife.
- Apply a second, wider, thin coat over the seams and screw heads.
- Let it dry.
- Apply a third thin coat if the seams are still visible.
- Thin coats are better than one thick coat; thick compound tends to crack and takes much longer to dry.

#### 8. Sand and check

- Use a fine sanding sponge, approximately 150–220 grit.
- Sand only until the repair feels smooth and the edges blend into the wall.
- Wear a dust mask and avoid sanding through the tape.
- Shine a flashlight across the wall from the side. Shadows will reveal ridges or low spots.
- Add a little more compound and repeat if needed.

#### 9. Prime and paint

- Wipe away dust with a slightly damp cloth and let the wall dry.
- Apply drywall primer over the repair. Primer prevents the patch from flashing through the paint.
- After the primer dries, paint with the matching wall color. A small test area is wise because the existing wall may have aged or the finish may be textured.

## Supplies to buy at Ace Hardware

### Drywall and repair materials

- One piece of drywall, matching the measured wall thickness (½-inch is common), at least large enough for both patches; a 2 ft × 2 ft piece may be enough if available, otherwise buy a larger sheet and have it cut or cut it at home.
- Premixed lightweight joint compound, small tub (approximately 3–4 quarts or 1 gallon; the small tub is usually enough).
- Paper drywall joint tape or self-adhesive fiberglass mesh tape, 2-inch wide.
- Drywall screws, fine-thread, length appropriate for the drywall thickness and framing (often 1¼ inch for ½-inch drywall).
- Drywall primer.
- Matching interior wall paint, if no leftover paint is available.

### Tools

- Tape measure.
- Pencil.
- 12-inch or longer straightedge, drywall square, or level.
- Utility knife with extra blades.
- 4-inch or 6-inch drywall taping knife.
- Drywall rasp or fine sanding sponge.
- Fine sanding sponge or 150–220 grit sandpaper.
- Screwdriver or drill/driver with a suitable bit.
- Flashlight.
- Drop cloth or plastic sheeting.
- Safety glasses.
- Dust mask or respirator rated for nuisance dust.
- Shop vacuum or dustpan and brush.

### Optional / useful

- Small scrap of drywall of the correct thickness for a test fit.
- Stud finder.
- Small level.
- Paint tray and small roller or brush.
- Disposable gloves.

## Quick answer to the practical questions

- **Do I need to cut the drywall?** Yes. Buy a larger piece and cut two patches to fit the two bays.
- **Do I need to sand it?** Yes, lightly, after the joint compound dries.
- **Do I need spackle?** Not necessarily. Premixed joint compound is the better choice for the seams; ordinary spackle is mainly for small holes.
- **Can I use the photo measurements to order?** Use 31 × 12½ inches as the overall opening estimate, but measure each bay and the wall thickness in person before ordering.
- **Should I order online now?** Wait until the thickness and the two bay measurements are confirmed. This avoids buying the wrong drywall or a piece that does not fit.

## Stop and get help if...

- You find wires, plumbing, mold, water damage, or rotten framing.
- The framing moves or appears structural.
- You cannot identify safe screw locations.
- The wall surface is textured and you are unsure how to match the texture.
- The patch keeps moving after fastening.
